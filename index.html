🧠 Aeromedical & Neurosurgical G-Force Analysis (FZ1073 Event)

Physiological, cerebral vascular, and biomechanical breakdown of what occupants and crew experienced during each phase.

1. Initial Dive Pushover 0.0 g to -0.4 g

Duration: Initial 4–6 seconds of the 29s plunge (34,000 ft down to 19,875 ft).
Biomechanical Effect: Complete weightlessness / negative G pushover. Unrestrained occupants, flight attendants, and loose cabin items floated freely off seats and struck the ceiling.
Neurosurgical / Hemodynamic: Reversal of hydrostatic venous gradient, cephalad venous congestion, acute facial engorgement, increased intracranial venous pressure, and "red-out" sensation.

2. Sharp Pullout & Steep Bank +3.5 g to +4.5 g

Duration: 3-second rapid pullout at 16,600 ft with steep bank turn (φ ≈ 45°–60°).
Biomechanical Effect: Occupants felt 3.5 to 4.5 times their normal body weight. A 75 kg human felt like 260–340 kg. Arm muscles required 4× force to lift, making the struggle for the control column extraordinarily strenuous.
Visual / Cerebral: Eye-level arterial pressure dropped by 70–95 mmHg. Blood pooled into the abdomen and lower extremities, causing progressive visual gray-out, peripheral visual constriction, and high risk of G-LOC (G-induced loss of consciousness).

3. Zoom Climb +1.4 g to +1.8 g

Duration: 45-second rapid ascent (16,600 ft → 21,725 ft).
Dynamics: Aircraft converted tremendous kinetic speed into potential altitude (averaging +6,833 ft/min climb). Occupants experienced moderate positive G load, stabilizing visual fields as G-forces eased back toward 1.2–1.4 g near the apogee.

4. Second Plunge & Level-off 0.4 g → +1.4 g → 1.0 g

Duration: 64-second second dive (21,725 ft down to 15,000 ft).
Resolution: Mild pushover at the second pitch-down followed by positive G arrest as the relief crew permanently stabilized controls and settled into level flight at 15,000 ft.
Airframe Note: The Boeing 737 is certified to +2.5 g clean limit. The +3.5 to +4.5 g pullout severely stressed the airframe, approaching its ultimate design limits.
